# KENSHO Card Visual Philosophy

## Luminous Precision

Luminous Precision treats the warranty card as a compact technical object, closer to a precision instrument than a flyer. Space is controlled, deliberate, and quiet. The card should feel like it was measured, calibrated, and assembled by someone with deep expertise, where every line, glow, and label has been placed through painstaking attention rather than decoration.

The visual language is blackened metal, cold light, and exact geometry. Blue light is used sparingly as a signal, not a background effect. Contrast should be high, edges crisp, and typography restrained. The composition must look meticulously crafted, with enough negative space that the QR code and product identity feel premium instead of crowded.

Scale creates hierarchy. The front face should feel like a brand artifact: a logo, a model name, and a warranty signal. The back face should behave like an activation instrument: a dominant QR code, a clear bilingual action, and a precise card ID. Text is essential-only and integrated as a visual element, never filler.

Craftsmanship is the point. The final card must look labored over with master-level execution, not assembled from generic cards. The grid, beams, and borders should be nearly invisible at first glance, then reveal depth when inspected. Nothing should overlap, crowd, or compete with the scan action.

The registered digital warranty view follows the same principle: it should resemble the customer-facing KENSHO warranty experience, not inherit another brand's certificate template. The warranty record is a verified technical credential, so its admin preview should preserve the same premium language customers see.
